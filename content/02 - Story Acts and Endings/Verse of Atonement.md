This is the **Redemption** ending. It is not a soft reset; it is the hardest path in the game, and it costs everything.

The player must have walked the Power path deeply—killing Pillars, consuming Echoes, growing the hole in their chest—but at their lowest point, they must have faced the **Discordant Hunger**: a manifestation of their own accumulated violence, a mirror that cannot be beaten by force. The player must have **surrendered**, letting the song of agony fall silent. The hole stabilised into an aching, empty scar. The player is weakened, nearly deaf, but awake.

Now, in Act III, knowing the truth that the world cannot be saved, the player turns away from the Demon of Sunder. The Demon, enraged, attacks before the player can fully surrender their stolen power. The player must fight the Demon with the tattered remnants of their strength—and, in a narrative twist, the very Echoes of those they killed appear as flickering, forgiving presences, briefly aiding them. The Demon is defeated, but as it dies, it brands the player with the **Mark of Dissonance**—a curse ensuring the player’s stolen song will eventually consume them. There is no cure.

The **Saint of Echoes** appears, drawn by the disturbance or perhaps following the player all along. The player’s soul is too scarred for Resonant Mirroring; it cannot be healed. But the Saint offers one final rite: **Soul Weaving**. Together, they enter the player’s fractured inner self. The Saint guides the player to gather every remaining shard of will, knowledge, and regret, and forge them into a single, transferable **Legacy Echo**. This process is fatal. The Saint, already broken, pours the last of their life into the ritual and passes away. The player’s body dissolves.

The Legacy Echo—a crystalline note containing the player’s entire journey, their sins, their remorse, their stubborn love—imprints onto the **Silent Child**, a young Dissonant the player may have ignored or threatened before. The child receives not just knowledge, but the weight of a life fully lived and fully regretted. They become the new Saint, carrying the player’s legacy forward.

In the final moments, the Silent Child stands at the edge of the breaking world, the Legacy Echo glowing within them. They do not sing alone; the player’s voice is a harmonic overtone in their throat. They conduct **The Gathering of Voices**—a smaller, more fragile choir than the Harmony ending, but one infused with hard‑won wisdom. The bubble bursts. The world dissolves. But the child’s note, carrying the player’s sacrifice, enters the Verse as a seed of courage.

**Epilogue:** The new universe begins with a single, clear voice—a child’s note, trembling but unafraid. It carries an echo of the love that was given at the last. The player’s name is forgotten, but the note they enabled is eternal. The cycle continues, and somewhere, in the fabric of the new song, there is a stubborn, quiet phrase that refuses to be silenced.

> *“…but it refused.”*
