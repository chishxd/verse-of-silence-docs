# Raw Thoughts on Characters & Dialogue

*This is not lore. This is me thinking out loud about what makes characters real, what makes dialogue hit, and what my game should feel like. I'm keeping this messy on purpose.*

---

I fucking love Sans. He is my ideal character. His smile feels silly and gentle in the Pacifist ending, but in Genocide, "you are gonna have a bad time." I still kneel to Toby Fox for that.

I dream of writing dialogue like:
- "I am afraid" from RDR2
- "But it refused"
- "Despite everything, it's still you"
- "You are gonna have a bad time"
- Doflamingo's speech at Marineford

Anyone can write the words "I am afraid." But when Arthur says it, I cried. The setting they set—the nun is still smiling and happy, telling him good news while he speaks of his TB. And everything Arthur does, helping out that captain (or Braithwaites?)—he understands the contrast of Dutch's ideals, which lead him to death from TB, and the importance of getting John out.

In Undertale, it was the epic battle against a superpower, but—despite the strongest calamity trying to kill us, the soul refused. Sans, a jolly character who loves his brother Papyrus, who is so innocent and pure—will become the worst fucking nightmare. It's absolutely beautiful.

That's what I need to do. Setting up characters from the BEGINNING, and THEN dialogue delivery. Shaping them into lore, not branching out of lore.

---

## Characters Are Bricks, Lore Is Mortar

The world is the foundation. The characters are the bricks. The lore is what holds everything together. But the dialogues will emerge from the character, not from me.

Even an NPC is the main character of their own story. The foundation is how characters think. The characters will have their own understanding. The lore will make them take decisions.

I can't write everything with that depth, and there should be unanswered questions. It MUST be messy. I love the mess my IRL world is. Nobody can figure it out. There will be some guys who just want to live their lives happily, some who ignore all this cosmic bullshit, some who just want to explore the world.

Lots of characters can make the player see the world differently—through basic dialogues, or side quests. Or some cities will tell their own tale, not the characters themselves. I fucking love environmental storytelling. It is beautiful.

---

## The World Is Just a Stage

The world is just a stage. Characters have their ideologies—either from learning, prejudice, or fed to them. The lore is discovered by the MC. The world is not scripted around them. The character gets information from various sources and interprets their own reality, their own truth, their own ideology, and their own ANCHOR.

Does the lore want them to protect the world by fighting everything in front of them? Or do they just hate the Chorus from prejudice? Do they want to accept cosmic reality and work for unification in the last times? Or just say it's bullshit and stay with the Dissonants?

---

## Dialogue Should Not Be Philosophical

A Chorus soldier might not say: "I believe order protects us from existential collapse."

He might instead say: "You people always destroy towns and call it freedom."

Common people won't know about all this cosmic stuff. They just know Dissonants are bad. And many Dissonants are, in fact, bad.

Kids are playing in a town, but they run into their house after they learn we are a Dissonant. The kid doesn't know why we are bad—it's their parents' teaching. Even on the Harmony path, until you prove you're not bad by doing something for the well-being of people, they won't acknowledge you. Kids will give you funny names. Old people will invite you for tea.

I love these little things from Pokémon. You can enter almost every house, even if they look the same. You still have to explore and talk to everyone. Not everyone has a story or anything. But they have funny quotes, or just rant about regular daily lives. Not everything is philosophical in my game, goddamnit.

---

## What the World Should FEEL Like

I want the world to feel like a place players are living in.

On the Harmony path, each and every creature seems significant. Every living thing deserves to live. They shall not be killed for existing. This makes the atrocities of war more critical. We never state this in the game, but that's how the environment should FEEL.

In Vagabond, Musashi was so strong. He never saw life as a precious gift, but as a thing to show dominance and hide his fear of not being relevant. That's what the farmer tells him... he DOES NOT SEE THE WEAK. That's why the Power route will be darker. The emotional anchor on this route is addiction to temporary power, Satisying the fear of not being great. But they are never being invincible. Weaker than a skinny monk. Because the one on the evil route is always under the fear of being replaced; like a beast in a den, ferocious but constantly hungry. This hunger ends up eating them.

---

## The Demon of Hatred (Needs a Better Name)

The Demon of Hatred (this boss name is from Sekiro, we need a better one) is so weak and powerless at the beginning. He was half dead from his hunger within. He sees the MC as a chance to feed upon. But deep inside, he was always afraid that WE would kill him if he doesn't seem useful.

That's why he guides us, making us ignore the world itself, being too self-centric.

He is the embodiment of what the player is becoming on the Power path. Not a cackling villain—a starving, terrified creature. His real name, if he even remembers it, should be something small. Something human. Something that reminds the player this thing was once a person.

## A Random Dialogue Idea

"This food, it is delicious."

The first sentence our character says out loud on the Redemption path. After killing and feasting upon others with hatred in mind, the food is now delicious. I thought of this randomly right now. I don't know if it means anything. Maybe it does.

## The Core Question

What is the single line of dialogue in my game that players will remember ten years from now?

I don't know yet. The characters will tell me. I am just the author. I don't know what they will say. It depends on the situation, the setup, the character's mentality during that moment.
